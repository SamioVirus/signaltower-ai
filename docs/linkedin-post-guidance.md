# LinkedIn Post Guidance

## Positioning

Write from the perspective of a senior technical program leader who makes AI useful inside real operating environments. The differentiator is not AI enthusiasm alone; it is the combination of infrastructure delivery, governance, stakeholder alignment, and practical automation.

Keep the main message consistent:

> I help teams turn complex infrastructure and AI initiatives into governed, measurable execution.

## Content Pillars

1. **Enterprise AI adoption and governance** — moving from pilots to production, readiness, evidence, human review, ownership, and value realization.
2. **Infrastructure and program delivery** — real estate, utilities, vendors, dependencies, portfolio governance, risk, and executive decisions.
3. **Practical workflow automation** — Gemini, Claude Code, Apps Script, APIs, reporting automation, and responsible team enablement.

## What the Current Results Suggest

The profile's most accessible operational-trust post reached substantially more people than the more abstract harness and control-loop posts. Treat this as a working hypothesis, not a permanent rule: lead with a recognizable business problem, then introduce the technical idea in plain language.

## Recommended Post Structure

Aim for 180–350 words with short paragraphs.

1. **Hook:** A direct question or tension a program leader recognizes.
2. **Business problem:** Explain the operational cost in one or two sentences.
3. **Concrete example:** Use a public, synthetic, or generalized scenario.
4. **Three lessons:** Keep each lesson short and action-oriented.
5. **Executive takeaway:** State what a leader should decide or change.
6. **Closing question:** Invite practitioners to compare approaches.

## Voice

- Practical, calm, and specific.
- Translate technical ideas into operating decisions.
- Show expertise through useful detail, not inflated claims.
- Prefer “Here is what worked” over “The future will change everything.”
- Use one strong example instead of a long tool list.
- Mention tools only when they support the business lesson.

## Confidentiality Guardrails

- Do not name internal programs, vendors, partners, systems, leaders, or customers without approval.
- Do not publish exact internal budgets, volumes, targets, performance results, architecture, roadmaps, controls, or strategy.
- Use ranges, composites, public information, or clearly labeled synthetic examples.
- Do not present a prototype as deployed production capability.
- Remove screenshots containing company data, browser tabs, email addresses, document names, or internal interfaces.
- Keep a human review step for every AI-assisted post.

## Visual Guidance

- Use one clean visual: a simple operating model, decision flow, dashboard crop, or three-part framework.
- Keep titles large enough to read on mobile.
- Avoid screenshots with dense interface detail unless the interface itself is the proof.
- Use the SignalTower visual language when relevant: dark navy, restrained blue accents, clear status hierarchy, and minimal copy.

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- The opening two lines make sense without expanding the post.
- The post contains one concrete business problem.
- The AI point connects to delivery, governance, or measurable value.
- Acronyms are explained or removed.
- Claims are accurate and appropriately scoped.
- Confidential information has been generalized.
- The post has one clear takeaway and one natural question.
- Links and visuals render correctly on mobile.

## Three Strong Next-Post Briefs

### 1. The executive report that should not take a day

Explain how program teams can automate data collection and first-draft reporting while preserving owners, exception review, and final human sign-off. Focus on decision speed rather than the tool stack.

### 2. AI transformation needs a delivery office, not just a lab

Describe the operating layer required between promising pilots and production: intake, sponsors, KPI baselines, readiness evidence, risk review, and escalation paths. Use SignalTower AI as a synthetic illustration.

### 3. What infrastructure delivery taught me about AI adoption

Connect dependencies in real estate, utilities, construction, and vendors to AI dependencies such as data ownership, controls, integration readiness, and business adoption. The thesis: execution systems matter as much as technical potential.
