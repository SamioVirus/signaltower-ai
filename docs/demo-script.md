# SignalTower AI Demo Script

Purpose: polished external presentation script.

## 30-Second Version

SignalTower AI is a synthetic AI commercialization control tower for regulated enterprises. The premise is that most organizations do not lack AI ideas; they struggle to move the right ideas from pilots into governed production. This demo shows how leaders can see which AI initiatives are ready, which are stuck, why they are stuck, and which decision could unlock the most value fastest.

## 60-Second Version

SignalTower AI is a portfolio layer for AI commercialization in regulated enterprises. Many AI ideas have potential value, but they often stall between business ownership, data readiness, governance, risk review, control evidence, and production monitoring.

The app shows an executive view of the AI portfolio, readiness scores, evidence requirements, repeated bottlenecks, and ranked leadership decisions. It is not trying to bypass governance. It shows how governance can become more visible, reusable, and accountable so leaders can convert AI demand into production value with appropriate control.

This is a generic outside-in prototype using synthetic data only. It contains no real institution, system, person or metric, and no company branding.

Worth adding if the audience is technical: none of the figures are entered. The data file records control evidence only; a tested engine derives readiness, blockers, gate verdicts, the ranked decisions and the forecast. That is what makes the what-if simulator possible.

## 2-Minute Version

SignalTower AI demonstrates an AI commercialization control tower for regulated enterprises. The thesis is that the bottleneck is not lack of AI ideas. The bottleneck is pilot-to-production congestion across value, data readiness, governance, risk, controls, ownership, and monitoring.

Start with the portfolio view. It shows active AI initiatives, their stage, value at stake, readiness, risk, and blockers. The goal is to give executives a clear view of where the AI portfolio stands.

Then move to Readiness. Readiness is treated as an operating question, not only a model question. The control matrix scores twelve controls per initiative — sponsor clarity, KPI baseline, data readiness, lineage, security, privacy, model risk, human oversight, monitoring, value tracking, and where applicable vendor risk and autonomy limits — weighted by risk tier. The vertical stripes in that matrix are the gaps several teams are each solving separately.

Next, open an Evidence Passport. Every initiative has one. It shows the evidence required to reach production, makes missing ownership visible before launch, and — in the score breakdown — shows exactly which control is costing how many points. It also surfaces control debt: evidence a stage required on the way in that was never approved.

Then show Bottlenecks and Executive Decisions. The point is to distinguish repeated operating-model constraints from one-off project issues and identify the leadership decision most likely to unlock value quickly.

Close on the 90-Day Model. The operating model focuses on inventory, governance templates, production conversion, and executive cadence. The prototype is synthetic and generic. It is not an internal bank tool and does not contain real company data or internal claims.

## Screen-By-Screen Walkthrough

## 1. Landing

Position the product as a synthetic executive control tower for governed AI commercialization. Emphasize the thesis: regulated enterprises have many AI ideas, but value is delayed when pilots cannot move through readiness, governance, and production controls.

## 2. Overview

Show the executive portfolio view. Point to the mix of initiatives, stages, risk levels, blockers, and value at stake. The key message is that leadership can see the whole AI portfolio instead of managing use cases through disconnected updates.

## 3. Readiness Board

Show readiness scoring and filters. Explain that readiness includes business ownership, KPI baseline, data readiness, lineage, privacy, model risk, human review, monitoring, and value tracking. The message is that production readiness is multidimensional.

## 4. Evidence Passport

Open the Evidence Passport for a representative use case. Walk through required evidence, missing evidence, ownership, and control status. The point is to make production evidence explicit before a use case is approved or scaled.

## 5. Bottlenecks

Show repeated blocker categories and value trapped by those blockers. Explain that this view helps leaders identify whether delays are caused by data lineage, control review capacity, ownership gaps, monitoring readiness, or other operating-model constraints.

## 6. Executive Decisions

Show ranked decisions. Explain that the app is designed to focus leadership on the decision that unlocks the most value fastest while preserving appropriate governance and control quality.

## 7. 90-Day Model

Close with the operating model. Walk through the 90-day sequence: inventory, standard evidence templates, focused production conversion, and an executive cadence. Reinforce that this is a generic synthetic prototype, not an internal bank tool.
